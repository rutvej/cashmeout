# 11 — Relationships, Marriage & Family Dynamics

Financial decisions do not happen in an emotional vacuum. Over a 10-year span, interpersonal commitments fundamentally transform spending, cashflow volatility, risk tolerance, and time availability.

---

## 1. Dating & Co-Living Arc (Years 2 to 4)

- **The Encounter Event (Year 2):** Player encounters a prospective partner. Initial dates cost $60 to $150 per outing (+5 Mental Health / Happiness).
- **Moving In Together (Year 3):**
  - *Option A (Co-Habitate):* Move into a shared apartment. Rent and broadband are split 50/50, reducing housing overhead by **-$350/mo**.
  - *Option B (Maintain Independence):* Retain personal lease; no risk of lifestyle friction, but higher recurring expenses.
- **Partner Profile Generation:**
  Each partner has a randomized economic archetype:
  - *Career Driven:* High salary ($3,500/mo), frequent work travel, independent savings.
  - *Creative / Freelance:* Variable income ($1,200 to $4,000/mo), high emotional warmth.
  - *High Spender:* Appreciates luxury travel and fine dining; elevates `lifestyleCreepCounter`.

---

## 2. Marriage & Joint Finances (Years 4 to 6)

### The Wedding Budget Dilemma
When engagement occurs, the couple must choose how to celebrate:
1. **Intimate City Hall Ceremony ($1,500):** Minimal financial impact; full capital preserved for house down payment or investments.
2. **Traditional Celebration ($8,000):** Modest banquet; parents may contribute $3,000.
3. **Grand Luxury Wedding ($22,000):** High social status, but requires exhausting liquid reserves or taking an unsecured personal wedding loan.

### Merged Financial Reality
- **Joint Household Inflow:** Partner's monthly earnings (typically $1,800 to $3,200) pool into household cash.
- **Hidden Debt Revelation:** 25% chance the partner enters the marriage carrying $5,000 to $18,000 in student or credit card debt that now impacts joint borrowing capacity.
- **Tax Filing Status:** Joint tax optimization unlocks standard married filing deductions.

## 3. Children & Dependent Care (Years 5 to 8)

When the family expands, the game's economic and time math undergoes a permanent structural shift:

| Stage | Financial Outlay | Daily Time Slot Impact | Health & Emotional Dynamics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Childbirth & Delivery** | $3,500 – $7,000 (Reduced by Health Insurance tier) | -1 slot for medical visits | +35 Happiness, Energy dip |
| **Infancy (Months 1–12)** | +$650/mo (Formula, diapers, clothes) | **-2 daily slots permanently** | Chronic sleep debt if partner working |
| **Toddler / Daycare (Age 1–4)** | +$950/mo in licensed preschool fees | -1 daily slot (Drop-off/pickup) | Illness events transfer from daycare |
| **Primary Schooling (Age 5+)** | +$400/mo public / +$1,200/mo private | Normal slots resume | Education savings goal prompts |

## 4. Extended Family Support & Parental Aging

Between Years 4 and 9, random filial obligation cards emerge:
1. **Parental Medical Crisis:** Aging parents face unexpected healthcare procedures.
   - *Full Support:* Transfer $3,000 to $6,000 in emergency capital (+family harmony, drains savings).
   - *Partial Support:* Transfer $1,000.
   - *Inability to Assist:* Drains mental health (-15) and strains family relations.
2. **Parental Co-Living:** Parent moves into spare bedroom; eliminates rent assistance, but adds daily care obligations.
